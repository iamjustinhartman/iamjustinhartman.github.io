On Wednesday I had a meeting with Rutger-Jan van Spaandonk who is the executive director and shareholder of the [Core Group](http://www.core.co.za/?ref=justhart.com). In case you don’t know Core Group are the official distributors for [Apple](http://www.apple.com/?ref=justhart.com), [Nintendo](http://www.nintendo.com/?ref=justhart.com) and [Tom Tom](http://www.tomtom.com/?ref=justhart.com) in South Africa.

RJ is an interesting man and he gave us a full status quo of where _his_ products are placed in the SA market which was fascinating. What was more interesting though was when he told [Gregor](http://groogle.co.za/?ref=justhart.com), [Carly](http://rantofnote.blogspot.com/?ref=justhart.com) and myself that [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com/?ref=justhart.com) owns a small stake in Apple Inc.

The big Apple vs. Microsoft war seems rather pointless after news of this so I decided I wanted to quantify this statement with some facts.

Turns out that at the 1997 Macworld Expo, Steve Jobs announced that Apple would be entering into partnership with Microsoft. Included in this was a five-year commitment from Microsoft to release Microsoft Office for Macintosh as well as an investment of 150,000 shares of Apple Series A, nonvoting, convertible preferred stock worth $150 million.

Steve had this to say to the audience.

> If we want to move forward and see Apple healthy and prospering again, we have to let go of a few things here. We have to let go of this notion that for Apple to win, Microsoft has to lose. We have to embrace a notion that for Apple to win, Apple has to do a really good job.
